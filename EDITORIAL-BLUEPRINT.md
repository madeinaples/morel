# Andrea Morel — Editorial Blueprint

## The promise
Andrea Morel is a pseudonym, not a fictional biography. A real author writes from lived experience, memory, observation and stories entrusted by other people. Names and identifying details may be changed for privacy. Original photographs belong to the author's archive. The pseudonym creates distance for honest writing; it is not intended to deceive the reader.

## Homepage rule
The homepage must answer three questions within seconds:
1. Who is Andrea Morel? — A declared pseudonym used by a real author.
2. What is this? — A narrative notebook about lived experience, contemporary life and entrusted stories.
3. How do I use it? — Enter through one of three collections, then read, listen or explore optional material without losing the narrative thread.

There must be one taxonomy, not two competing sets of three cards.

## The three collections
### 01 — Andrea's Stories
First-person lived experience: memory, relationships, places, desire, ageing, solitude, work, departures and returns.

### 02 — Unfiltered Thoughts
The author's direct voice: observations, questions, cultural and psychological reflections, arguments and unfinished thoughts. These pieces may respond to a story or stand alone.

### 03 — Other People's Stories
Real encounters, confidences and lives entrusted to the author, transformed into narrative with identifying details changed where necessary.

Every article belongs to one primary collection. Tags may describe themes, but must never become a second navigation system.

## MOREL Threads
The three collections orient the reader; MOREL Threads connect individual pieces across them. Every new article must answer two questions before publication: which existing thought does it emerge from, and which thought does it open next?

Use “Continue the Thought” (or the natural Italian equivalent) instead of a generic related-articles label. A connection must be editorially real, not merely based on a shared keyword. If a piece has no honest place in the current map, keep it as a draft and withhold it temporarily rather than forcing a link.

International versions must not assume that readers know Italian television figures, public personalities, programmes, places or cultural references. Add only the context needed to understand why the reference matters, naturally and without turning the article into an explainer.

When a Small Code is conceived through the visual language of a painter, that visual relationship belongs inside the article as well as on the collection page. The image must remain editorial, properly captioned and accessible; the painter’s name must not function as decoration alone.

## Story-page rhythm
The story remains the centre. Optional modules can deepen it but must never be compulsory or repeat the same content.

Recommended order:
- Story title, context and reading time
- Main text
- Optional original photograph or small gallery
- Optional audio: a reflection/conversation inspired by the story, not an audio duplicate of the article
- Optional Author's Note: what remains unresolved, a question, or a personal afterthought
- Optional Interlude: recipe, object, place, document, memory fragment or other sensory pause when genuinely connected
- One question for the reader when useful
- Continue reading: 1–3 related pieces from the three collections

## Audio rule
Audio should add another perspective. It can be an author reflection, a structured conversation, a question explored from several sides, or a contextual note. AI-generated or synthetic voices must be disclosed clearly. Audio should be playable inline with a title, duration, short description and transcript where practical.

## Photography rule
Photography is evidence of a lived world, not a fabricated biography. Original images may appear inside stories or in the photographic archive. A photograph should be used when it contributes place, memory, atmosphere or documentary context.

## Interludes
Recipes and other pauses are not a fourth content category. They are editorial interludes attached to a story or reading path. Their purpose is rhythm: to let the reader stop, listen, look, cook, consider or respond before continuing.

## Tone
Clear before clever. Intimate without pretending intimacy. Literary without obscurity. Honest about pseudonym, memory, privacy and synthetic media. The reader should never need to decode the architecture of the site.

## Product principle
One story, several possible ways to experience it: read, listen, look, reflect. The interface reveals only what exists for that story. Empty modules never appear.
