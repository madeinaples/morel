Behind the Wall

Sometimes we need a barrier in order to get closer.

Author’s note: this story explores intimacy, vulnerability and consent between adults. Some details have been changed to protect the privacy of those involved.

It was Sunday afternoon.

One of those suspended hours when the city slows down without actually stopping.

A bus passed outside. The window gave a slight shiver.

I hadn’t opened that app in a while. I was scrolling without much attention when I stopped at a profile.

He said he was straight. He was looking for women. Trans women. Not men.

I moved on.

There wasn’t much to interpret.

I wasn’t what he was looking for.

Ten minutes later, a message arrived.

It was him.

He wrote politely. Almost cautiously.

He did not seem troubled so much by what he wanted as by everything surrounding it.

Being seen.

Looking.

Having to explain.

Perhaps even having to explain it to himself.

He wanted there to be some distance. Some protection. Something between us.

I didn’t ask why.

There are questions which, asked too early, do not help us know someone. They merely force that person to defend themselves more carefully.

I agreed.

When he arrived, we barely greeted each other.

We had already spoken enough to know where the boundaries were.

No one had to persuade anyone.

No one had to prove anything.

The door closed.

For a few seconds I heard only the small sounds two people make when they are trying to behave as though an unusual situation is perfectly ordinary.

A step.

The fabric of a jacket.

A chair moving a few centimetres.

Then silence.

There was a wall between us.

What it was made of hardly mattered.

It could have been wood.

Fabric.

Darkness.

Fear.

Sometimes the strongest walls need no bricks at all.

He needed it.

And I decided to leave it there.

In the dark, I began noticing the small things.

The ones I probably would not have registered in daylight.

A change in his breathing.

A movement of the hand, almost involuntary.

It stopped at once, as though he himself noticed it only after it had happened.

I didn’t know what it meant.

And I didn’t try to decide for him.

Breathing is a strange thing.

By day it belongs to the background noise of the body.

In darkness it can become a confession.

You can control a sentence.

You can prepare an answer.

You can decide where to look.

Breathing is less disciplined.

Sometimes it tells the truth before we are ready to.

The hand moved again.

This time more slowly.

I couldn’t tell whether he was reaching for something or simply checking that I was still there.

I stayed still.

In daylight, a gesture can be only a gesture.

In the dark it becomes a question.

And sometimes the answer is not to do something. It is simply not to step back.

For a few seconds neither of us did anything else.

It was probably the quietest moment of the whole encounter.

And perhaps the one in which we said the most.

The wall kept the images back.

It did not keep everything else back.

Not the breathing.

Not the hesitation.

Not an involuntary movement.

Not that tiny shift in distance I might never have noticed in daylight.

He had built a barrier in order to feel protected.

But walls are never completely impermeable.

Something always gets through.

At some point I stopped thinking about how strange the situation was.

I don’t remember when.

The tension simply changed shape.

What had first felt like distance began to feel like attention.

The wall was still there.

It had not become lower.

It had not collapsed.

And yet it stopped feeling like something that divided us.

We hardly spoke.

We didn’t need to.

There were smaller signals.

A pause.

A held breath.

The way someone moves closer and then stops.

The way they wait.

Trust does not always arrive with a declaration.

Sometimes it arrives like this.

In tiny amounts.

Almost invisible.

Then I started hearing the street again.

That was when I realised I had stopped hearing it for several minutes.

He was near the door.

We looked at each other.

For the first time, properly.

Or perhaps it was only the first time I felt as though we did.

He didn’t say much.

Neither did I.

Some experiences become smaller when you try to explain them too quickly.

The door closed behind him.

I stayed alone.

The wall was still there.

The room was the same.

And yet something felt slightly out of place.

Not an object.

Perception.

Later, a message arrived.

He thanked me.

For the discretion.

For respecting what he had asked for.

And for something he described as far more intense than he had expected.

I read it twice.

What struck me was the paradox.

He had arrived asking for a barrier.

And that very barrier had allowed something to pass through.

A week later, he wrote again.

This time to talk.

Not about the encounter.

About other things.

Work.

Life.

Writing.

Thoughts.

The conversation began moving in a direction neither of us had planned.

The wall was no longer there.

Or perhaps it had simply changed form.

For a long time I thought intimacy began when two people managed to lower their defences.

I’m no longer so sure.

Some defences are necessary.

At least at first.

They are how we move closer without feeling completely exposed.

The problem is not always the wall.

It is what the wall cannot contain.

Sounds pass through.

Warmth passes through.

Hesitation passes through.

That small movement of the hand no one planned passes through.

A changing breath passes through.

Even that almost imperceptible moment passes through, when someone stops asking whether they can trust you and decides, for a few seconds, that they can.

I don’t know exactly what he was looking for that afternoon.

I never asked him.

Perhaps he would not have been able to explain it.

Perhaps he was looking for an experience.

An answer.

A confirmation.

Perhaps simply a space in which he did not immediately have to know who to be.

It doesn’t matter.

Some questions work better when they remain open.

All I know is that he arrived with a wall.

And that wall remained between us until the end.

We did not tear it down.

We didn’t need to.

It simply became permeable enough to let something true pass through.

There are people we know for years and never quite manage to reach.

And then there are strangers who cross our lives for very little time and, without saying much at all, manage to show us something we had forgotten.

Perhaps because they do not ask to stay.

Perhaps because we have not yet had time to build the parts we usually perform.

Or perhaps because, sometimes, darkness lets us see the things that light distracts us from.

Later another bus passed.

The window trembled in exactly the same way.

The room was identical.

I wasn’t.

That afternoon two strangers did not tear down any wall.

Each remained on his own side.

But for a few minutes the wall stopped being a boundary.

It became something else.

A place through which silence passed.

Breathing.

Hesitation.

And that rare kind of trust that asks for no promise.

Only presence.

Perhaps neither of us knew exactly what we were looking for.

And perhaps that was precisely what made it possible.

Not everything needs to be named.

Sometimes it is enough to know that, on the other side,

someone was there.
