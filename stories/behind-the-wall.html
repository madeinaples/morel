Behind the Wall

A story about anonymity, desire, and the unexpected ways people recognise each other.

Author’s note: This story contains mature sexual themes and describes a consensual encounter between adults. Some details have been changed to protect the privacy of those involved.

It was three o’clock on a Sunday afternoon, and I wasn’t looking for anything in particular.

I hadn’t opened that dating site for almost two months. I logged in more out of boredom than desire, with the distracted curiosity of someone opening a door while already assuming there will probably be no one on the other side.

I looked through a few profiles.

Then I saw his.

He described himself as straight. He was looking for women and trans women. His photographs revealed only fragments: a large, well-trained body and a handsome face—enough to make me pause for a few seconds longer than necessary.

I didn’t message him.

I simply thought, Why would he ever be interested in me?

I wasn’t a woman. I wasn’t trans. And I had no intention of transforming myself, not even grammatically, to attract his attention.

I kept scrolling.

Ten minutes later, a message appeared.

It was him.

I sat up straighter in my chair.

Not because his request was particularly unusual. On dating sites, the unusual has a remarkably short life. After a few years, almost everything begins to feel like routine administration.

It was the way he asked.

He wanted to know whether I would enjoy giving oral sex to a straight man.

He didn’t assume I would. He didn’t speak with the confidence of someone who considered his presence a favour in itself.

He asked.

Politely.

It may sound like very little, but on dating sites, kindness is often the rarest form of eroticism.

I said yes.

Then he added a condition.

He could only do it through a glory hole, or in a completely dark room. He had never had an experience with a man, and the thought of meeting one directly—seeing him, being seen, fully acknowledging what he was doing—made him uncomfortable.

A wall, I thought, would solve the problem.

Unfortunately, I didn’t have one.

I looked around the room.

The base of an old sofa frame—a large rectangular structure destined for the tip—had been leaning against a wall for days.

I placed it across the bedroom doorway, covered it with a sheet of black rubber and cut an opening in the centre.

It was not exactly a masterpiece of engineering.

It looked more like the result of a collaboration between a very tired interior designer and a man with too much time on his hands on a Sunday afternoon.

But it worked.

I sent him a photograph.

Done.

I’m not sure which of us was more surprised.

He thanked me before he had even arrived. Not only for agreeing, but for making the effort to prepare something especially for him.

His gratitude struck me.

People rarely thank you in advance in sexual chats. More often, they treat desire like an order that has already been approved, and other people’s bodies like a service permanently available.

He didn’t.

He kept asking.

He kept checking that everything was all right.

He kept being kind.

Perhaps that was what made me feel safe.

Not the wall.

Him.

He arrived on time.

When I heard the knock, I briefly wondered what on earth I was doing.

Until a few minutes earlier, everything had existed only inside a screen: a profile, several photographs, a handful of sentences exchanged with a stranger.

Now that stranger was standing outside my door.

He entered without properly seeing my face.

We did not exchange a single word.

Not out of embarrassment.

My voice would have broken the film he was creating in his mind. He needed everything to remain suspended: without a face, without a name, without definitions.

I understood and remained silent.

I did not want to interrupt the fantasy or remind him who was standing on the other side of the sheet.

The wall offered him anonymity.

I tried to offer him attention.

For a moment, I looked through the opening.

The photographs had not lied.

He really was a handsome man: tall, broad, athletic. The kind of man I would never have imagined noticing me in a crowded room.

And yet there he was.

Behind a makeshift wall built from the base of an old sofa.

Life, when it chooses, has a rather sophisticated sense of humour.

I could not see his face.

I didn’t know what expression he wore or how far he was allowing himself to surrender to the experience.

I could understand only through his body.

The way it tightened, relaxed and responded. The small involuntary movements. The barely audible sounds.

Perhaps he was holding them back so as not to disturb the silence.

Perhaps he did not want to hear too clearly how much pleasure he was experiencing.

I could not know.

I didn’t need to.

Without the possibility of looking into his face, his body became his only language.

And I listened to him with my hands.

I wasn’t trying to impress him.

I wasn’t proving anything.

I followed his responses and allowed them to guide me.

Perhaps this is what many people forget when they think about pleasure: they become so focused on what they are doing that they stop listening to the person they are doing it to.

When it was over, there were no words.

Only the quiet sound of him moving as he dressed, and then the door closing behind him.

I remained alone in the room.

The old sofa frame was still resting against the doorway, the black sheet slightly out of place. In the ordinary afternoon light, it looked far less mysterious.

Almost ridiculous.

An object destined for the tip had become, for less than an hour, the boundary between what a man desired and what he was ready to admit.

Then my phone lit up.

He thanked me for the way I had welcomed him. For the discretion. For the fact that I had asked for nothing in return.

I had told him from the beginning that he owed me nothing.

I wasn’t expecting him to reciprocate.

I had chosen to give him that experience and, in a way that was difficult to explain, the pleasure he felt had already given something back to me.

It wasn’t selfless generosity.

It was participation.

Some people still confuse a sexual role with a person’s character.

They assume that someone who gives pleasure is passive, and that being passive means being submissive.

It does not.

A role describes what someone chooses to do in a particular moment. It does not reveal who they are, what they are worth or how much power they possess.

I was there because I had chosen to be there. My pleasure also came from his. And had I not wanted it, no amount of beauty, insistence or fantasy could have persuaded me.

That Sunday, I had not surrendered to someone.

I had followed an instinct.

I assumed the story was over.

A fantasy fulfilled.

A Sunday afternoon slightly more unusual than most.

An old sofa frame.

A black sheet.

The end.

A week later, he messaged me again.

This time, the hesitancy of his first message had disappeared.

He simply asked whether I was free.

I told him I was out and would be home later.

That afternoon, he wrote again.

Are you free? Can I come over?

I said yes. I also mentioned that I would soon be leaving and would be away for about twenty days.

His reply came almost immediately.

Oh, really? For how long?

It was a simple question, almost an ordinary one.

And yet it caught my attention.

For the first time, he wasn’t merely asking whether the wall was available.

He was asking where I would be.

He arrived on time.

The structure was still there, leaning across the bedroom doorway like a makeshift stage set that no one had quite had the courage to dismantle.

Again, we did not exchange a single word.

Silence was still part of the experience.

It wasn’t embarrassment.

It was consistency.

He needed to continue creating his own film without my voice returning him to something he was not yet ready to confront directly.

So I gave him silence.

This time, he lay beneath the sheet, covered from the waist upwards.

I could not see his face.

I could read only his body.

The tension.

The release.

The involuntary movements.

The sounds he held back.

And I listened to him with my hands.

When it was over, the silence remained intact.

I heard him move behind the sheet, dress and leave the room.

Not long afterwards, his message arrived.

Honestly, I’ve never had those sensations during oral. I have no idea what you did, but it was so good.

I read it twice.

I hadn’t done anything I considered mysterious.

No trick.

No secret technique.

I had simply paid attention.

Perhaps it wasn’t what I had done.

Perhaps it was the way I had listened to what he could not tell me.

He was handsome and athletic, probably accustomed to being looked at and desired.

But being desired does not necessarily mean being listened to.

Not even with someone’s hands.

I told him that when I felt a connection, I put passion and effort into what I did. Not because I felt obliged to please someone, but because the other person’s pleasure became part of my own.

He replied that he had felt it.

That I was clearly a giver.

Then he told me he appreciated the fact that I had welcomed a complete stranger into my home and made sure he felt comfortable.

He wrote:

A safe place.

Until then, I had thought I had built him only a wall.

I had not considered that, from his side, the wall might also have felt like shelter.

A place where he was not required to explain himself, choose a label or confront a part of himself before he was ready.

The wall allowed him to hide.

But it did not make him less human.

In encounters like that, it is easy to reduce someone to a body, a role, a fantasy to be consumed.

Anonymity can erase a face and a name.

It should never erase respect.

I could not offer him my voice.

But I could offer him attention, discretion and the certainty that, even behind that wall, he would remain a person.

We continued messaging.

The conversation gradually moved away from sex and towards questions that were simple, but no less legitimate for being so.

I told him I had a personal blog.

Perhaps, by reading what I wrote, he might understand a little more about me. He might not find definitive answers, but perhaps he would find something useful while exploring the questions he was beginning to ask himself.

I sent him the link to Andrea Morel’s website.

I explained only that the man in the photograph was not me.

It was a way to protect myself.

And, in part, to protect the people who entered my stories.

The words, however, were mine.

They did not need to borrow someone else’s face.

He read the most recent piece first, the one about bodies, age and the ways we learn—or fail to learn—to inhabit ourselves.

That was when he told me about his work.

He worked with men, many of them in their mid-fifties, often obsessed with their appearance and struggling with time, desire and the fear of no longer being enough.

He said what I had written felt consistent with what he saw every day.

It was not a generic compliment.

He had recognised something.

Then he kept reading.

He told me he had also enjoyed the story about Vinarium.

Finally, he wrote:

Love your writing style.

A little later, he added:

You understand human connection.

I stared at those words.

He had not opened the website merely to be polite.

He had followed the thread.

From bodies to people.

From insecurity to stories.

From the way I had listened to him behind the wall to the way I tried to listen to others on the page.

Perhaps they were not two different qualities.

Perhaps I did both in the same way.

By listening.

Then another message arrived.

He wrote that the world needed people who could express themselves in a way others could relate to, so they would not feel alone or broken.

He added that many people struggled to express themselves.

Himself included.

What made me truly happy was not only the compliment.

It was discovering that I had not been wrong about him.

From his very first message, I had sensed something different. He was direct, but never arrogant. Curious, but never intrusive. Behind a very explicit request, I had noticed a tenderness rarely found in dating chats.

I had trusted that instinct.

Now I knew it had not betrayed me.

I was pleased that he had wanted to read my stories.

But more than that, I was pleased that he had found something of himself in them.

Perhaps that was the confirmation I had needed.

Writing does not mean distributing definitive answers.

I have no doctrines to offer and no lives to save.

I can only tell the truth about what I have lived, honestly enough that someone might pause, recognise themselves and perhaps formulate a question they had never before been able to put into words.

The answers, when they come, belong to the reader.

At most, my stories can open a door.

Or create a small ripple.

A stone dropped into water has no way of knowing how far the circles it creates will travel.

Perhaps they will disappear almost immediately.

Perhaps they will reach someone on the opposite bank.

That evening, I had the feeling that one of my small stones had finally touched the water.

And that was enough.
