Loneliness Isn’t Always About Being Alone

The duty to be grateful. The right to recognise what is not good for us.

Loneliness has an excellent publicity department.

It is usually shown with rain on a window, a single lamp and someone staring into the middle distance with very expensive sadness.

Real loneliness is often less cinematic.

Sometimes it is simply living in a place that does not feel like yours.

You can have a roof, heating, furniture, safety and every practical reason to say: I should be happy here.

But happiness is not an inventory.

The duty to be grateful

Gratitude is a good duty.

It reminds us not to treat everything as owed.

But gratitude can become a cage when we use it to force ourselves to stay.

We can be grateful for what we have and recognise, at the same time, that it is not good for us.

When I moved, I tried to make the place work. Lamps. Objects. Small rituals. The sort of negotiations we conduct with existence when we want a room to feel like home.

But a well-placed lamp cannot manufacture belonging.

Not knowing where to go

Feeling trapped does not always mean being unable to leave.

Sometimes it means not knowing where to go.

That uncertainty can keep us somewhere long after the practical reasons have expired.

We call it stability. Maturity. Being sensible.

Sometimes it is.

Sometimes we have simply become very good at enduring.

Places affect us more than we like to admit.

Some calm us. Some wake us up. Some slowly dim us.

Buildings are rarely vindictive, which is useful. But that does not mean every place is neutral.

The right to change place

Adapting is not always a virtue.

Resisting is not always strength.

If we praise endurance without asking what it is costing us, we eventually start calling every long-lasting surrender maturity.

The duty to appreciate what we have should not erase the right to look for what makes us feel alive.

That does not mean running at the first discomfort.

It means noticing when we are living as a performance of the person for whom this place would be perfect.

Home is not only where we sleep

A home should protect us without separating us from life.

It should make it easier to return to ourselves, not harder.

Not everything adequate is right. Not everything safe is home. And not everything we are grateful for has to last forever.

Perhaps the point is not to escape.

It is to recognise when the time has come to move.
