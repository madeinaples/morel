Editorial manifesto · Andrea Morel

Manifesto

Experience, people, memory. And photographs that do not always need a camera.

I am not here to teach anyone how to live.

I am interested in something much simpler and much more complicated: looking.

People.
Cities.
Encounters.
Departures.
What we desire and what we run from.
The things we do without knowing that, years later, they will mean something completely different.

Andrea Morel begins here.

With experiences lived, observed, imagined, or entrusted by someone who decided to tell their story.

I am not looking for perfect characters.

I am interested in contradictions.

We can be brave and afraid.
Generous and selfish.
In love and desperate to escape.
Convinced we have understood everything, only to discover ten years later that we understood almost nothing.

That is usually where a story begins.

I write about relationships, desire, friendship, sex, memory, travel, cities, mistakes, returns, and those apparently insignificant things that sometimes change a life.

Without turning them into lessons.

I prefer questions to answers.

And I prefer to leave a few doors open.

The photographs

Photographs are part of this project too.

Some are real photographs: places I have passed through, moments that remained, imperfect images because they belong to life, not to an advertising campaign.

Other photographs do not exist.

The reader creates them.

A dimly lit room.
A blue door.
A breath.
A hand moving almost involuntarily.
A street perhaps all of us have walked down, even if it had a different name.

If I can make you see it without showing it to you, that is a photograph.

Perhaps it is even the kind that interests me most.

Looking a second time

Not everything I tell needs an ending.

Life rarely gives us one so neatly arranged.

Some people stay.
Others disappear.
Some return when it would frankly have been better to lose their number.

And sometimes we are the ones who return.

This space is for that too: looking a second time.

Not to correct the past.

To understand what we can see today that we were not yet capable of seeing then.

With irony, when possible.

With seriousness, when necessary.

Without being ashamed of contradictions.

And without turning life into a moral lesson.

Because Andrea Morel does not want to tell you what you should see.

He only wants to place something in front of you.

In the end, you take the photograph.
