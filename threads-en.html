MOREL · NARRATIVE MAP

Threads

They are not categories. They are threads.

MOREL is not built like a neat archive, but like a web of thoughts.

Some texts begin with a song. Others with a photograph, a house left behind, a sentence too small to become an article and too insistent to disappear.

That is what the Threads are for: following a thought as it changes shape.

Here, an article does not really end when the page ends. It may continue in another voice, an image, an object or a sideways memory. Every text comes from something and opens onto something else.

You do not need to read everything in order. You only need to choose which thread to let draw you in.

01

POP MEMORY

Pop Memory

Pop culture was not an escape. It was where the future tried on its stage costumes.

I do not write as a music critic. I write as someone who watched pop stop being mere spectacle and become a prediction.

Pop Memory looks at songs, videos, icons, bodies and characters as early signals of the present: identity as performance, fame as construction, desire as strategy, constant reinvention.

The Seventies, Eighties and Nineties were not simply decades of style. They were a fierce, imperfect transitional zone, often more human than the present knows how to allow. Today many of those forms return, but often colder, more calculated, more compatible with the algorithm.

MOREL does not look back to say yesterday was better. It looks back to understand what the present inherited, what it mistranslated, and what it keeps repeating without remembering where it came from.

02

CHANGING SELVES

Changing Selves

We are not obliged to remain faithful to the version of ourselves that others learned to recognise.

Changing is not always an escape. Sometimes it is simply the moment we stop inhabiting a version of ourselves that has become too small.

Changing Selves brings together texts about change, identity, and the versions of ourselves we have inhabited, defended, betrayed or simply allowed to expire.

Changing your mind sounds simple. In fact it is often a small domestic war. Every time we change, someone remains attached to our previous version: the more predictable one, the more convenient one, the one that did not disturb the story too much.

This Thread does not celebrate change as a motivational slogan. It looks at the less photographable side of transformation: guilt, irony, discomfort, the fear of seeming inconsistent, the need to explain yourself, and the moment when staying the same would be much easier but much less true.

03

DESIRE & DISTANCE

Desire & Distance

Desire rarely comes through the front door. More often it stays on the threshold, pretending not to know what it wants.

Not everything that brings us close brings us together. And not everything that takes us apart sets us free.

Desire & Distance brings together texts on relationships, intimacy, waiting, encounters that do not quite know what they will become, and the modern forms of desire.

It does not speak of love as a solution. It does not speak of sex as performance. It does not speak of solitude as an orderly tragedy to be fixed by the next paragraph.

Instead, it watches that ambiguous space where people brush against one another, seek one another, avoid one another, write too late, disappear too soon, and remain inside a chat longer than they stayed in a room.

Here, intimacy is not explained too much. It is left in view.

04

CITIES WE CARRY

Cities We Carry

Some places do not remain behind us. They keep walking with us, even after we have changed address.

We do not live only in the houses we inhabit. We also live in the ones we have left, the ones we desired, and the ones where we never managed to become fully ourselves.

Cities We Carry brings together texts and photographs about the places that run through us: houses, streets, rooms, bridges, neighbourhoods, cities we loved badly, cities we left too early, cities where we stayed longer than expected.

It is not a travel section. It is not an urban guide. It is not a melancholic postcard.

It is where space becomes memory.

A photograph can be the beginning of a story. A window can become a question. A station can contain more biography than an About page. MOREL does not look at places to describe them. It looks at them to understand what they have held of us.

05

SMALL CODES

Small Codes

Sometimes a tiny crack says more than a confession.

Not everything needs to become a grand story. Some things are enough when they remain small, as long as they keep unsettling us.

Small Codes gathers MOREL's miniatures: sentences, objects, brief scenes, small everyday mishaps that seem to say nothing and yet open a fissure.

A cup of tea. A surviving sock. A sentence spoken too late. An object left out of place. A confusion nobody had the good taste to explain.

Small Codes are not short articles because there was no time to write a long one. They are another form: quicker, more oblique, closer to a note left on the table by someone who did not really want to leave.
